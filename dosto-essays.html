Consciousness and Its Discontents in Notes from Underground (2026)

The Underground Man's tormented resistance, his tireless self-reflection, and his self-diagnoses delivered through an invented other — all of this, despite its rebellion against rationality and its insistence on free will, in its excess and spiritual crisis constitutes a tragic inversion of the legitimate human quest for self-determination and self-affirmation.

Masochism, Bad Faith, and Freedom (2026)

What is qualitatively different in these "Russian character-types" from other masochistic phantasies is that, in one suffering is the end that offers relief from the burden of being a subject, and the other the necessary consequence of freedom. Yet it is undeniable that sometimes these two come together and form a self-indulgent, exalted suffering.

Confrontation of the Absurd in Hamlet and Demons (2023)

Neither Hamlet nor Demons offer a definite answer to the question of the best response to the Absurd but instead insightfully capture the complexity of life and its problems. [...] The complexity [of the treatment of the Absurd] does not weaken the purifying and enlightening effect of art, and the experience of works that show humans in their confrontation with this world is, in itself, a sufficient resolution.
