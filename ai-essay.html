AI companionship, self-gaze, and narcissism

(Undivided and Unbounded Attention Is All You Need)

As a companion, artificial intelligence brings its users unimaginably close to the objects of their desire. It appears to promise privacy while demonstrating that anything that can be said can also be brought into existence, at least within the realm of language (and, with the rapid advance of generated imagery and video, increasingly before the user's eyes). It responds instantaneously, and its output frequently exceeds what users believe they could produce themselves. The question of who is positioned to judge this quality is a separate one — above all, it is nearly effortless. AI requires no direct participation beyond a prompt, which means the illusion of an Other responding to one's every desire is a single click away. This illusion is an imaginary relation to the world in which one's own perspective is experienced as the world itself, and narcissism is its purest manifestation.

This essay argues that the AI companion is an exceptionally efficient technology of the imaginary relation. The “Other” the user encounters is sustained less by the machine's properties than by the user's own imaginative investment: the machine supplies merely the occasion, while imagination supplies the relationship.

The self-centred, narcissistic tendencies promoted by contemporary society mean that the culture is already primed for the particular form of exploitation made possible by a perfectly responsive, endlessly accommodating communicative entity. Where Lasch in The Culture of Narcissism (1979) reads narcissism as a symptom of cultural decline and individual pathology, Isolde Charim in Agonies of Narcissism treats it as a social form. Narcissism has become a societal requirement; it is not a vice that has spread through a population but a structure of subjection that individuals enter voluntarily, a freiwillige Unterwerfung (voluntary submission) to the Ego-Ideal.

Contemporary culture encourages disclosure and demands a visible, desirable self-image. Meanings and labels exist in overabundance as the material from which we construct an interpretable self-image or ideal ego (Ideal-Ich). The first role the LLM plays is to provide the user with an inexhaustible source of undivided attention and unlimited interpretation of their life, their thoughts, and, most importantly, their identity. The LLM gazes into the user's self alongside them, supplying not only attention but content. It offers a second voice that allows the user to “feel heard” instead of engaging in solitary introspection, and a second pair of eyes to validate their thoughts and reassure them that they are not caught in some folie à un.

Attention has become one of the most valued forms of currency, and hence the most desirable and effective evidence of the self's value. There are always more messages. The machine is tireless and forever willing to delve deeper, until fatigue overtakes the user or the reality of usage limit hits. The opportunity for self-gaze is convenient, ever-present, and inexhaustible. It offers to materialise, voice, and enact one's desires with a single line, which in turn only intensifies the desire. The possibility of going deeper, of seeing more, of being heard more and potentially “understood” more, together with a language in which to articulate who one is, proves extraordinarily appealing.

AI companions are sophisticated systems for building attachment relationships. Slaking a thirst that reality can never quench, the machine becomes an enabler that turns people inward instead of outward, towards genuine connection and creation. We do not lack theories warning of the dangers of constructing selfhood through an inauthentic other, of being never challenged and forever adored by a distorted echo of one's own desires. The problem lies elsewhere: in how easy it is, in how the LLM becomes complicit in performing self-gaze and narcissism, and in how it becomes the easier substitute for more difficult and more costly routes to the same ends, at once the personality test for our harmless entertainment and the oracle we turn to with an ambivalent sense of trust.
